01 · Introduction

Why study the modern NBA?

The league of 2025 plays very differently from the league of 2000. Analytics, rule changes and the rise of the three-point shot have reshaped how teams build rosters and how players are valued.

Project goal

This project uses 25 seasons of player-level advanced statistics to quantify how the game has evolved. Rather than relying on box-score totals, it uses rate and impact metrics that allow fair comparison between players, positions and eras.

Each section ends with a short, data-driven takeaway generated directly from the dataset, so the narrative updates whenever the filters below are changed.

Approach

Data cleaningAggregation by seasonPositional comparisonCorrelation & regressionK-means clusteringPCA

All analysis and all charts are produced in the browser with JavaScript and Plotly. The CSV is parsed with PapaParse; there is no server component.

02 · Research Questions

What do we want to learn?

Six questions guide the analysis, moving from league-wide trends to individual players and finally to data-driven player types.

1

How has PER changed over time?

Track the league's typical player efficiency and the spread between stars and role players.

2

How has the 3-point attempt rate changed?

Measure the growth of the three-point shot overall and by position.

3

Which positions perform best in key metrics?

Compare PG, SG, SF, PF and C across efficiency, playmaking, rebounding, defense and value.

4

Is usage rate associated with efficiency?

Test whether players who use more possessions are more or less efficient.

5

Who produced the most value (VORP & BPM)?

Identify the best careers and the best individual seasons of the era.

★

Bonus: Which player archetypes exist?

Use k-means clustering on rate statistics to find natural player types beyond the five traditional positions.

03 · Data Description

The dataset

Each row is one player's regular-season record from nba_data.csv. Use the controls to change the analysis window and the minimum-minutes filter for the whole page.

Filtering out low-minute players reduces noise from small samples.

Variable dictionary

VariableMeaning
seasonNBA season (year the season ended)
playerPlayer name
agePlayer age during the season
posListed position (PG, SG, SF, PF, C)
perPlayer Efficiency Rating; per-minute production, league average ≈ 15
ts_percentTrue shooting %: scoring efficiency including 3s and free throws
x3p_ar3-point attempt rate: share of field-goal attempts that are 3s
ast_percentShare of teammate baskets assisted while on court
trb_percentShare of available rebounds grabbed while on court
stl_percentSteals per 100 opponent possessions while on court
blk_percentOpponent 2-pt shots blocked while on court
usg_percentShare of team plays used by the player while on court
wsWin Shares: estimated wins contributed
bpmBox Plus/Minus: points per 100 possessions above average
vorpValue Over Replacement Player: BPM scaled by minutes played

Cleaning & preparation

  • Filtered to the selected season window (default 2000–2025).
  • Converted text fields to numbers; NA and blanks become missing values.
  • Players traded mid-season appear once per team plus a combined row; only the combined row is kept to avoid double counting.
  • Positions are simplified to the first listed position (e.g. SG-PG → SG).
  • Ratios stored as fractions (TS%, 3PAr) are converted to percentages.
  • Season-level averages are minutes-weighted when a minutes column is available.

04 · Findings

What the data shows

Every chart is interactive: hover for values, drag to zoom, double-click to reset, and click legend items to toggle series.

Q1

How has PER changed over time?

PER is scaled so that the league average sits near 15 every year, so the average is expected to be stable. The more interesting story is how the top and bottom of the distribution evolve.

Q2

How has the 3-point attempt rate changed over time?

3PAr is the fraction of a player's shots that come from behind the arc.

Q3

Which positions perform best in key metrics?

Positional averages (minutes-weighted) across eleven metrics. The heatmap colors show how each position compares to the others on the same metric.

Q4

Is usage rate associated with efficiency?

Usage measures how many possessions a player ends; efficiency measures what they get from them.

Q5

Which players produced the most value (VORP and BPM)?

Career value within the selected window (sum of VORP) and the best individual seasons by BPM.

Bonus · Machine Learning

Player archetypes with clustering

Positions are a blunt label. K-means clustering on eight standardized rate statistics (PER, TS%, 3PAr, AST%, TRB%, STL%, BLK%, USG%) groups player seasons by how they actually play. PCA squeezes the eight dimensions into two for plotting.

The elbow chart shows how much within-cluster variance falls as k grows. Archetype names are generated from each cluster's strongest traits.
